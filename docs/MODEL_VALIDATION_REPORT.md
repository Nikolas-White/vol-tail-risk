**Overall assessment.** The final rating for each model is set mechanically by a rule
fixed before the results were seen (section 10). The validator's qualitative view:

- The *estimation and forecasting code* is fit for purpose. The from-scratch GARCH and HAR estimators reconcile with `arch` and `statsmodels` (on a 6,000-day simulated sample GARCH parameters agree to about 1e-5; HAR coefficients to 1e-8; the tests enforce 2e-3 on GARCH α/β). - The *main model risk* lies not in the code but in (i) proxy measurement error in volatility evaluation, (ii) parameter instability across regimes, and (iii) normal-tailed models in the 99% tail. These are covered in sections 6–8.

## 2. Purpose and intended use

**Intended use.** Daily risk monitoring of liquid single-instrument exposures: (a) a
forecast of next-day and next-week volatility for position sizing and limits, and (b)
1-day 99% and 97.5% VaR and ES for risk reporting and backtesting.

**Out of scope.** Portfolio aggregation and correlations; multi-day (10-day) regulatory
VaR; intraday risk; illiquid instruments; options and other non-linear payoffs; stressed
VaR calibration; any trading signal.

**Users.** A risk analyst reviewing daily limit utilisation, and a validation analyst
monitoring backtest performance.

**Data-quality controls.** Missing or non-positive closes, weekend rows, stale holiday
rows, spike-and-revert bad ticks, and inconsistent OHLC are each detected, counted and
logged (`data/processed/data_quality.json`). No price is forward-filled.

**Data limitations (validator's view).**
1. Free data has no audit trail. Yahoo revises history and occasionally publishes bad highs and lows (FX in particular), so range-based proxies inherit those errors.
2. The FX "daily close" in the free feed is not a standard fixing time, and asset closes are not synchronous. That is why cross-asset features are lagged a full day.
3. Index levels (`^GSPC`, `^GSPTSE`) exclude dividends. This is immaterial for 1-day risk but matters for long-horizon return studies.
4. Bitcoin's history is short (from 2014), so out-of-sample evaluation starts in October 2016 and includes no GFC period.

**Volatility proxies.** True variance is unobservable. Evaluation uses r² (unbiased but
noisy) and a Garman-Klass proxy (more efficient, but needs rescaling for the overnight
gap, done with a past-only ratio).

## 5. Conceptual soundness

| Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Volatility clustering** | GARCH-type, EWMA and HAR models capture it by construction. Rolling SD and HS adapt only as the window turns over. This is conceptually weak in fast regime changes, and section 7 tests exactly that. |
| **Leverage effect** | Present in equity data (a known stylised fact). GJR and EGARCH model it; the XGBoost features include min(r, 0). Symmetric GARCH and EWMA ignore it. |
| **Fat tails** | Normal innovations understate 99% quantiles for most financial returns. Student-t, FHS and EVT address this in different ways: t is parametric, FHS empirical, EVT extrapolates the tail. EVT depends on threshold choice (fixed at the 90th percentile, about 75+ exceedances in the first window). Sensitivity to the threshold is a recommendation (section 9). |
| **Stationarity and parameter stability** | GARCH assumes constant parameters. Monthly refits on an expanding window partially accommodate drift, but they adapt slowly after structural change. Parameter paths are monitored (figure 7 in RESULTS.md). |
| **Estimation** | MLE for the GARCH family (well established; the Student-t likelihood is robust to tails). OLS for HAR, with log transform and smearing correction. XGBoost tuned only on the burn-in window, with a small grid to limit overfitting. |
| **Proxy choice for evaluation** | Using an unbiased proxy (r²) with a robust loss (QLIKE) is the textbook-correct approach (Patton 2011). The Garman-Klass results are a robustness check. |
| **Backtesting framework** | Covers frequency (Kupiec), independence (Christoffersen), regulatory zones (Basel), ES magnitude (Z2) and joint scoring (FZ0). Multiple testing is handled through pass shares rather than single p-values. |
| **Known conceptual gap** | All models are univariate. There is no portfolio aggregation, no liquidity horizon, and no scenario or stress overlay. |

**Why models fail in stress, and what to look for in the tables above:**
- *Historical simulation* reacts only when crisis days enter its window. Its exceedances bunch at the **start** of a stress window: see the "first 20 days" column in RESULTS.md section 5. A longer window is more stable but slower to react.
- *Normal-tailed models* (EWMA, GARCH-n) can have the right volatility and still exceed 99% VaR too often, because the normal 1% quantile is too close to the mean for fat-tailed returns.
- *Parameter drift*: GARCH persistence and Student-t degrees of freedom shift across regimes (RESULTS.md figure 7). Monthly refits partly absorb this.

### 7.5 Benchmarking and sensitivity
Every model is compared with simple benchmarks (rolling SD, EWMA, HS). A sophisticated
model that cannot beat EWMA or HS in the MCS is not adding value. Sensitivities **not
yet run**, recommended in section 9: estimation window (expanding vs rolling), EVT
threshold, HS window, refit frequency.

## 8. Limitations and model risk

1. **Proxy risk.** Volatility "accuracy" is measured against noisy proxies. Rankings with r² are robust in expectation but have low power. Range proxies need a scale adjustment that is itself estimated.
2. **Backtest, not live.** Forecasts are produced with information available at each close, but nobody traded or reported on them in real time. Data revisions and operational delays are absent.
3. **Daily data only.** No intraday realized variance, so volatility at the daily horizon cannot be measured precisely. Intraday jumps are invisible.
4. **Univariate.** No correlations, so no portfolio VaR. Diversification and correlation breakdown in crises are not captured.
5. **Regime definitions are ex post.** They are fixed calendar windows used only for evaluation slicing, but they were chosen with hindsight about which periods were stressful.
6. **Multiple testing.** Several thousand test statistics are produced, so some rejections are expected by chance. Ratings use pass shares.
7. **Model selection risk.** The model list and hyperparameter grid were chosen by the developer from the literature, before seeing results. Even so, comparing 13 models invites "best of many" optimism. MCS membership is the guard against that.
8. **Free-data integrity**, as in section 4.

## 10. Final ratings

Rule (pre-registered in `src/voltail/evaluation/ratings.py`): a VaR/ES cell
(model × asset × level) passes if Kupiec p ≥ 0.05, Christoffersen CC p ≥ 0.05, Z2
p ≥ 0.05 and the full-sample Basel-style zone is not red. **Satisfactory**: pass share

## 11. Ongoing monitoring plan

| Metric | Frequency | Amber trigger | Red trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 99% VaR exceptions, rolling 250 days | Daily | 5–9 (yellow) | ≥ 10 (red) | Amber: investigate. Red: escalate and consider a model change or capital add-on |
| Christoffersen independence p-value, rolling 500 days | Monthly | < 0.05 | < 0.01 | Check for clustering; consider faster-reacting variants |
| Z2 ES test, rolling 500 days | Monthly | p < 0.05 | p < 0.01 | Review tail calibration (t ν, EVT ξ) |
| QLIKE vs EWMA benchmark, rolling 250 days | Monthly | Model worse than benchmark | Worse and DM-significant | Re-estimate; challenge model choice |
| Parameter drift (GARCH persistence, ν, EVT ξ) | At each refit | Move > 2 SE month on month | Persistence ≥ 0.999 or ξ ≥ 0.9 | Review the estimation window; check data errors |
| Data checks (stale rows, bad ticks, range flags) | Daily | Any new drop | > 3 drops in a week | Reconcile against a second source |
| Forecast staleness (`/health` days_since_last_close) | Daily | > 3 days | > 5 days | Check the scheduled refresh job |
| Full revalidation | Annual, or after a red trigger | | | Re-run this report |
